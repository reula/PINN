Black-hole puncture CCZ4 PINN: constraints, mass and drift

Work of 20–26 October 2026 · O. Reula, with Claude · same code and problem as the 10–19 October summary (J. Massó): one 6×40 network per 0.25 M window, single puncture m = 0.5, t ≤ 10 M, SSBroyden throughout. All results graded against the collaborators' 1D spherical reference (dr = 0.005) and the Misner–Sharp mass, not the training loss. Draft: the control run that attributes the last finding is running.
Bottom line. The trained networks are not Ricci-flat near the horizon, and the residual loss cannot see why: a constraint-violating solution still satisfies the CCZ4 evolution equations. Adding the CCZ4 constraints Θ and Zⁱ (zero for a true solution) to the loss fixes the black hole: to t = 10 M its mass stays within 0.35 % (final settings without it: +7–8 %), the lapse at the puncture within 0.003 of the reference, and the lapse error near the hole drops 10×. What the penalty does not change is a mass deficit that leaves the hole at t ≈ 2 and travels outward with the gauge pulse, growing to about −20 % (sphere average) at r ≈ 10–12 by t = 10. It is the same with or without the penalty. Point-symmetric sampling clouds stop the puncture drift (25× less).

1. The loss is not the accuracy; the reference is

With the final settings the code reproduces the 10–19 Oct numbers against the reference: α error at the puncture 0.022 / 0.032 at t = 0.25 / 1 (theirs 0.019 / 0.024), 5× better than the defaults (0.108 / 0.153). On fresh points never trained on, the loss is 5–10× higher than the value logged in training, and the logged loss falls (1e-6 → 2e-7) over a stretch where the mass error of the same run grows to +28 %. Every result below is therefore judged by the reference and by the mass, which in vacuum spherical symmetry must equal M at every radius and in any slicing (checked exact to 4e-15 on the initial data).

2. Where the mass error comes from

Each window's network is evaluated at its start and end, so the error at any radius splits exactly into the initial-data fit, the growth inside windows, and the jumps at the hand-offs between windows. In single_a at t = 10 (r = 1): initial −1e-4, inside windows +0.30, hand-offs −0.015. So the error is built inside the windows, not inherited at the hand-offs, and its radial density is positive only at r ≲ 0.3, around the horizon; the integral carries it outward.

The cause is direct. We rebuilt the 4D metric and the Z4 vector Zμ from the network's CCZ4 fields (Alic et al. mapping; exact Schwarzschild passes it to 7e-15) and evaluated the Z4 field equations Eμν = Rμν + ∇μZν + ∇νZμ. In window 1, the rms of E is 0.2 at r ∈ [0.3, 0.7), 7e-3 at [0.7, 1.5) and 4e-4 at [3, 6): the network's spacetime is not Ricci-flat at the ~5 % level around the horizon, while the CCZ4 residual there is 2.4e-4. The residual loss sees the evolution equations, and these admit solutions with Θ, Zⁱ ≠ 0, which carry wrong mass. Only the initial data rule them out.

3. Constraint penalties

Two ways to rule them out, each a term added to the loss, zero on the true solution:

  • Direct (--w_constraint W): W·mean(Θ² + |Zⁱ|²) over the interior points. Cheap.
  • Contracted Bianchi (--w_bianchi W): for any metric and covector, ∇μ(Eμν − ½gμνE) = □Zν + RνμZμ. Z obeys a linear wave equation sourced only by the divergence of the field-equation residual, which is the part of the error that feeds the constraint modes. The term W·mean(B²), B = □Z + R·Z, on 4096 core points, checked to 5e-16 on random metrics. It needs third derivatives of the network and costs ~35× the residual per point.

At t = 2 (8 windows), both cut the core errors ~10× against the same run without them: core α error 1.2e-2 → 7e-4 (direct) / 1.1e-3 (Bianchi), max Θ 0.45 → 3e-4 / 1.4e-2. Continued to t = 10:

The direct term is the clear choice for the hole: Θ falls 10⁴×, and the lapse and the mass at the puncture match the reference. The Bianchi term, the more physical idea, does less at the weight tried and makes the hole too light (−6 %).

4. What is left: an outgoing mass deficit

The maps separate two errors. Inside the outgoing front the hole's mass is what the penalties fix: steadily too heavy without them (+27 %), right with the direct term, uniformly too light with the Bianchi term. The second error is common to all three runs: a deficit band leaves the hole at t ≈ 2 and moves out along the path of the gauge pulse, reaching −18 to −21 % at r ≈ 10–12 by t = 10. This matches the 10–19 Oct observation (“up to 17 % at r ≈ 4–12 where the gauge pulses pass”). The lapse error and Θ follow the same outgoing paths. Along a single axis the deficit can be twice its sphere average (−36 % on the ±x rays of the direct-penalty run), so it is not spherical. Where the pulse travels (r ≈ 5–15) every run rests on the sparse uniform sample, since the core and focus clouds sit at the hole, so that region is the first candidate. The control run (final settings, no penalty, running) will show whether the final settings alone change it.

5. The puncture drift is a sampling effect

A single puncture should stay at the origin; in the defaults run it reaches 0.13 by t = 10. With puncture tracking off the push at the origin is 10× smaller. Holding the clouds at the origin only halves it, while drawing every cloud in point-reflected pairs about its centre stops it:

The seed is the random lopsidedness within each cloud, which tracking amplifies. Symmetric clouds leave the mass error unchanged; drift and mass are separate problems. The penalised runs drift 10× less without symmetric clouds.
