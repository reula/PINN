1. One code, faster derivatives

The three received versions (bundle base, multi-GPU _gpus, Oct-16 optimiser update) were merged by a three-way merge over a reconstructed ancestor; with default flags the merged script reproduces the Oct-16 loss history byte for byte. The merged Crunch of 17 Oct is installed (bit-identical with its options off) and scale_invariant is now on by default: without it QN freezes at loss ~1e-11 (39 of 40 updates skipped when the loss is scaled by 1e-8). A batched second-order forward pass for the residual's derivatives (--deriv_mode taylor) agrees with autodiff to 1e-15 and is 1.75× faster on the A30. The production size fits one A30: 9.5 of 24 GB, ~25 min per window.

Which part of the loss belongs to which optimiser. Each DSGNAR stretch starts on a fresh sample, ~10× above QN's last value on its own sample, and removes 30–50 % of it in its half of the window's time.

3. DSGNAR here and in the paper's own code (ldc/ldc.ipynb)

The lid-driven-cavity notebook drives the authors' implementation (vendored in Evolution_try/vendor/dsgnar_pinn) step by step; ours is Stationary's re-implementation. Same algorithm, but these differences matter for comparing results:

So the branch test is not the paper's use of DSGNAR: a third of its sketch, a tenth of its iterations, and a slower Jacobian. It answers “does a short DSGNAR phase help QN?” (no), not “is DSGNAR a better primary optimiser here?”.

4. The puncture drifts through the sampling, and the mass invariant degrades

A single puncture should not move; in single_a the tracked position reaches (−0.121, 0.039) by t = 10 M. The shift at the origin, which drives the tracker, measures the push directly (a χ minimum cannot: χ ~ (2r/m)⁴ is flat to the network's error within r ≈ 0.05):

run (t ≤ 4 M)βˣ(0), t = 2.75βˣ(0), t = 4reading

tracking only (--chi_bg_mode physical)3.5e-33.0e-2

The likely loop: the focused point clouds follow the track, a small asymmetric β moves the track, the off-centre clouds teach a larger asymmetric β. One seed per setting. The Misner–Sharp mass (verified exact, 4e-15, on the initial data) grows alike in all three runs to t = 4 M and to 35 % by 10 M in single_a, mostly at r ≈ 0.3–2, while the logged loss falls from 1e-6 to 2e-7; the 10–19 Oct summary reports +7–8 % with its final settings, so most of our larger error is the first-baseline configuration.

Next steps

  • Rerun the baseline with the final settings of the 10–19 Oct summary (48k core points, 24k QN steps) and judge every later test by the mass invariant, not the loss.
  • DSGNAR the paper's way: build its Jacobian by rows (≥ 2.5× cheaper here), s ≈ d/3, as the primary optimiser of a window, graded on held-out points and the mass.
  • Sampling that follows a puncture without becoming lopsided: first, tracking on with the clouds held at the origin; then clouds drawn symmetrically about each tracked centre, for the binary.
